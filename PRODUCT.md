# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Product designers, frontend developers, and care-service practitioners who need to make a concrete interaction decision in a sensitive or high-stakes service. They need a pattern they can evaluate and implement, along with its limits.

## Product Purpose

Help a visitor describe a design task, receive a relevant pattern brief, try a working example, and check the decision before shipping. Success means visitors can explain why a pattern fits, implement its core behavior, and identify what still needs validation.

## Current proof

The Harbor referral comparison is the case used to test disclosure, separate sharing decisions, cancel-and-return, and an automatic pause prompt. The prototype is built. Sessions with care workers have not been run. See `research/harbor-referral.md`. The public README matches this limit.

## Brand Personality

Clear, respectful, and evidence-aware. The site should convey competence and agency without making clinical promises.

## Anti-references

- Emotional-state scoring that suggests a diagnosis or validated risk measurement
- Demo controls that imply an action occurred when nothing happened
- Claims that a quick exit clears all browser history
- A visual style that uses muted colour as proof of psychological safety
- Legal lists that imply every listed law applies to every service

## Design Principles

1. Start with the person's task and its consequences.
2. Show choice, refusal, and recovery as working paths.
3. State exactly what the interface does with data and browser history.
4. Keep guidance specific, source-linked, and conditional on context.
5. Treat each pattern as a hypothesis to test with its intended users.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Use WCAG 2.2 AA as the design target. Support keyboard use, visible focus, reduced motion, readable text, mobile layouts, and specific content warnings. Validate language and behavior with people from the communities and services where patterns will be used.
