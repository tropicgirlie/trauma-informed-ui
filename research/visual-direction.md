# Visual direction: a working field guide

## Why this direction

The earlier demo used familiar dashboard cues: a full-height sidebar, blue action colour, rounded white cards, and repeated panels. Those cues made a useful tool feel like a template. The redesign treats the site as an annotated field guide: authored, task-led, and clear about sources and limits.

This is a visual and editorial hypothesis, not a claim that the new look improves safety. Test comprehension, task completion, and refusal or recovery paths with practitioners and service users.

## Research cues

- **Beyond the average interface.** The [iF Design Trend Report 2026](https://ifdesign.com/en/if-magazine/newsroom/if-design-trend-report-2026-key-design-trends-and-countertrends-shaping-innovation-ai-sustainability-and-future-cities-insights-for-designers-companies-and-strategists) describes an “Age of Average” and renewed interest in design with a distinct point of view. We take that as a reason to build an identifiable editorial system, not to add novelty for its own sake.
- **Object-led discovery.** [Cooper Hewitt's digitisation project](https://www.cooperhewitt.org/2016/08/31/unprecedented-access-to-cooper-hewitts-collection-following-18-month-mass-digitization-effort/) pairs objects with metadata and paths for discovery. We translate this into short field notes with place, date, source, and one question to carry into a design task.
- **Provenance is part of the interface.** [Digital Benin](https://digitalbenin.org/) connects objects and histories across dispersed collections and makes colonial catalogue language visible. We place historical context and the source beside each example, instead of extracting motifs or presenting a fact as universal design advice.
- **Interpretation should retain context.** [UNESCO's ethics guidance for intangible cultural heritage](https://ich.unesco.org/en/ethics-and-ich-00866) emphasises community roles and avoiding decontextualisation. The site uses examples as questions, not as proof that one pattern works across cultures or contexts.

## Applied design rules

1. **One task route:** situation → brief → working example → release checks.
2. **Editorial hierarchy:** a compact masthead, Newsreader display type, IBM Plex Sans interface type, strong rules, and numbered routes.
3. **Source-linked notes:** each historical or public-service example retains its provenance and asks a specific design question.
4. **Restrained colour:** dark ink and cool paper carry the reading experience; signal yellow marks identity and route; deep teal marks interactive guidance. Colour does not communicate state alone.
5. **Working product first:** the Decision Guide remains above the field notes, and examples remain interactive.
6. **No decorative borrowing:** historical and cultural examples do not become illustrations, textures, or badges detached from their origin.

## Cases used in the site

- [JapanGov on Seiichi Miyake's tactile paving](https://www.japan.go.jp/kizuna/2021/08/spirit_rooted_in_tokyo_1964_paralympics.html): a prompt to check whether important information is available through more than one channel.
- [Digital Benin](https://digitalbenin.org/): a prompt to make provenance and context visible around sensitive material.
- [GOV.UK Exit this page](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/exit-this-page/): a prompt to design the seconds and browser consequences after a person leaves.
